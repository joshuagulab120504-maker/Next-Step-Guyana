# The Guyana Youth Opportunities Guide
### Competitions, clubs, sports, arts, STEM, leadership and service — what exists, how it works, and how to actually get in
*Companion to the career pathway guide. Same confidence-flag discipline: ✅ = sourced from an official/organisational source or credible news report; 🟡 = general guidance or pattern-matched, confirm before publishing. Verified against search-accessible sources only — no page was opened in full, so treat dates and figures as "confirm before acting."*

---

## WHY THIS LAYER MATTERS TO THE SITE

The career guide answers *"what do I need to pass?"* This one answers *"what do I do between now and then?"* — and for most students that's the difference between a plan they follow and a plan they abandon.

Three practical reasons to build this in:

1. **Most of these have no grade requirement.** A Form 2 student with mediocre marks can win a national calypso title, represent Guyana at a robotics olympiad, or earn a President's Youth Award Gold. These are the opportunities that build confidence *before* CSEC decides anything.
2. **They're the evidence layer for scholarships and overseas applications.** Chevening, Fulbright, and most competitive scholarships explicitly weigh leadership and community impact. A student who only has grades has an incomplete application. This is where that content comes from.
3. **They surface interests earlier than a subject-choice form does.** A student who discovers they love debating in Form 2 has real information for their Form 3 stream decision. The competitions *are* career exploration.

---

## THE MINISTRY'S OWN FRAMEWORK: THE FIVE PRIORITIES PROGRAMME ✅

This is the single most useful organising idea found in this research, and the site should probably adopt it directly. The Ministry of Education's stated goal — led by the **Unit of Allied Arts** — is that **every single high school child should exit school with:**

1. Heavy exposure to **at least one sport**
2. **One musical instrument**
3. **One TVET subject**
4. A **strong sense of volunteerism**
5. **At least one foreign language**

That's an official, government-stated five-point checklist for a well-rounded student. It maps almost perfectly onto a progress tracker: five slots, and the site can show a student which they've filled and which are empty. It's far more motivating than a subject checklist, and it comes with the Ministry's own authority behind it rather than being something your site invented.

# CATEGORY 1: DEBATE, SPEECH & PUBLIC SPEAKING

## J.O.F. Haynes Memorial Secondary Schools' Debating Competition ✅
**The flagship national schools debate competition**, run by the **Ministry of Education's Secondary Sector**.

**How it actually works** (from the 2025–2026 cycle):
- **Launched late April** at the NCERD Auditorium; runs through **June**
- Knockout rounds — Round One, Round Two, semi-finals, final — with byes awarded where numbers are uneven
- Debates are **broadcast on the Guyana Learning Channel**, so participation carries real visibility
- Moots are current and substantive. Actual 2026 motions included: whether a **Universal Basic Income funded from oil revenues** is the most effective way to eliminate poverty in Guyana; whether Guyanese secondary schools should **replace textbooks with mandatory digital tablets**; whether the shift to **Smart Classrooms creates a digital divide** outweighing the benefits; and whether the **Safe Road Intelligent System** deters accidents better than physical police presence.

**Who competes:** Notably *not* just the traditional Georgetown elite schools. The 2026 field included **Mackenzie High (winner), Three Miles Secondary (runner-up), North Ruimveldt, Annai Secondary, New Amsterdam Secondary, Santa Rosa Secondary, West Demerara Secondary** — hinterland and out-of-town schools reaching the late rounds. **Nephlyah Farrel of Mackenzie High** was 2026 Best Debater.

**What you get:** Plaques for finalist schools, a Best Debater award, and — for all debaters from both finalist teams plus their teachers and coaches — gift vouchers, cellphones, accessories, and stationery.

**How to get in:** Through your school. Ask the Secondary Sector coordinator or your English/Social Studies teacher about entry. If your school isn't participating, that's the thing to push for — the barrier is usually institutional inertia, not eligibility.

## World Schools Debating Championships (WSDC) 🟡
The global high-school championship, 70+ national teams, one team per nation, World Schools format (3v3, eight-minute speeches plus a four-minute reply). **No confirmation was found that Guyana currently fields a WSDC team** — this is a genuine gap worth investigating, because the J.O.F. Haynes competition is exactly the kind of domestic circuit that would feed one. If Guyana doesn't currently compete, that's an opportunity, not a dead end: the QatarDebate **International Schools Debating Championship** explicitly allows individuals and schools from communities *without* a national championship to register directly through its website.

## Other accessible international debate routes 🟡
- **Cosmos Championship** — a virtual global debating platform running competitions almost weekly, split into Junior (13–15) and Senior (16–19) age groups, open to students anywhere. Low barrier, no travel.
- **World Individual Debating and Public Speaking Championships** — individual rather than team-ranked, with four events (Debate, Impromptu Speaking, Persuasive Speaking, Interpretive Reading).

# CATEGORY 2: STEM, ROBOTICS & CODING

This is the most developed youth-opportunity ecosystem in Guyana, largely because of oil-sector and IDB funding.

## STEMGuyana ✅
The central organisation in this space — founded by **Karen Abrams**, operating in partnership with the Ministry of Education.

**What it runs:**
- **After-school clubs and "Learning Pods"** — year-round programmes in local communities
- **Robotics teams**, **AI programmes**, drone work, and online learning platforms
- **Intensive 2-week holiday programmes** covering robotics, coding, AI, and digital design for **ages 8–18**
- A **clubhouse** for hands-on coding/robotics/drone experience
- Coach and mentor certification, plus professional development for teachers and parents

**Reach:** Delivers STEM and robotics training across **Regions 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 10**, including out-of-school youth, and claims reach across all 10 regions with thousands of students and hundreds of trained mentors.

**Where it leads:** This is the pipeline to international competition. A team of five young innovators plus coaches from the **National Learning Pods Program** represented Guyana at the **International Greenwich Olympiad (IGO) in London**. STEMGuyana's 2024 robotics team developed a **solar panel cleaning robot prototype** in response to an IDB challenge — real engineering work with commercial application as Guyana's solar capacity expands.

**Student voices worth showing on the site** (from STEMGuyana's own materials): Keoma, 13, an Indigenous student from Region 9 — *"I never thought I could build a robot in my village."* Aaliya, 14, East Coast Demerara, on going from not knowing what AI was to building smart games. This is the emotional proof that the geography barrier is beatable.

## National STEAM Fair ✅
Run by the **Ministry of Education** as a **biennial national fair**, preceded by **regional STEAM fairs in every region** that feed into it.

**How it works:**
- **Three-day event**, students from **primary through tertiary**, across all education districts
- **Five participant categories:** nursery schools, primary schools, secondary schools, practical instruction centres, and observer groups
- Features interactive exhibits, hands-on workshops, and student projects addressing real-world problems, with emphasis on sustainability, entrepreneurship, and technology-driven thinking
- Scale reference: one regional fair at Queen's College displayed **85 projects**
- Recent theme: *"Transforming Education through Innovation and STEAM"*

**How to get in:** Via your school's entry into the **regional** fair first — the regional fairs are the qualifying layer, so that's the actual entry point, not the national event.

## National Scratch Coding Competition ✅
A genuinely accessible entry point, and the clearest evidence that geography isn't destiny in Guyana's STEM scene.

**How it works:** Teams of students in **grades 1–10** create a Scratch animation project on a set theme (one edition: researching the effects of climate change on their community or country). **80+ teams from 8 regions** participated in the second edition; **50+ came from STEMGuyana's learning pods**.

**Who won:** Georgetown International Academy took first, but the regional top teams came from **Port Kaituma (Region 1), Aurora (Region 2), Bell West (Region 3), Central Mahaicony (Region 5), West Canje (Region 6), Bartica (Region 7), and Tabatinga (Region 9)** — with **Lake Mainstay** taking a "Director's Trophy" for excellence and citizenship. Teams like *4TGirls* and *Team Sunflower* placing nationally from small communities is exactly the story to put in front of a hesitant student.

**Prizes:** Certificates for all students; cash prizes for top 3 overall teams and coaches; certificates and medals for top regional teams. Sponsored variously by the **IDB, Tullow Oil, GTT**, and members of the Guyanese diaspora.

## Caribbean STEM Olympiads (CSO) ✅
Run by the **Caribbean Science Foundation** — the most credentialed regional STEM competition available to Guyanese students.

**Structure:** Three categories — **Math Olympiad, Computer Coding Olympiad, Robotics & Electronics Olympiad** — each at three levels (I, II, III), making **nine competitive events** total.

**Team rules:** Math Olympiad up to 3 members; Coding and Robotics up to 4. **You can enter as an independent competitor, not affiliated with any school** — a critical detail for a student whose school won't organise entry. For Math and Robotics all team members must be in the same room; for **Coding, team members may be in different locations, even different countries**, and citizens of different countries may share a team.

**Difficulty, stated honestly by the organisers:** Questions deliberately **won't always align with the CSEC or CAPE syllabus** by age level, because the Foundation is trying to bridge Caribbean maths standards toward leading countries'. Expect to study or work outside the classroom to compete. Typically it's exceptional teams that reach finals.

**Guyanese track record:** **Titianna Wells, Tomika Wallace and Rihanna Taylor of Queen's College** won **silver in Level 2 of the 2025 Computer Coding Olympiad** for a conservation science and ecology project, *"Tides of Tomorrow."*

**Explicit stated benefit:** The Foundation states outright that it aims to give medalists **a competitive advantage on university applications.**

## ExxonMobil / Greater Guyana Initiative STEM programmes ✅
- **After-school STEM programmes for Grades 7–11**, funded by ExxonMobil Guyana in collaboration with **Volunteer Youth Corps** — lessons, workshops, STEM seminars, and robotics across multiple schools, reaching hundreds of students annually. VYC's version has run since 2012 with Ministry of Education endorsement, covering English, Human & Social Biology, Biology, Mathematics, Integrated Science, Physics and Chemistry through interactive activities.
- **RASSR camps** (Regional Accelerator for STEM Students' Readiness) — annual camps under the Greater Guyana Initiative for students from all regions, tied to strengthening tertiary STEM at UG.
- **STEM and robotics clubs in Special Education Needs schools**, via partnership with the **Guyana Council of Organisations for Persons with Disabilities (GCOPD)** — genuinely inclusive provision worth flagging for students with disabilities.

## St. George's School of Sciences ✅
Guyana's **first secondary school dedicated to scientific learning**, digital tools, and advanced STEM curricula, recently commissioned by government. Worth tracking as an admission target for STEM-focused students — confirm current intake criteria directly.

# CATEGORY 3: CULTURE, PERFORMING & CREATIVE ARTS

## National Children's Mashramani Competitions ✅
**The largest youth participation event in Guyana**, and the single best entry point for a student whose talent isn't academic.

**How the pipeline works** — this three-tier structure is the important part:
1. **District/regional competitions** first (e.g. Georgetown Education District launches in January; Region 2 holds its leg at Anna Regina)
2. **Winners advance to the National Children's Mashramani Competitions** at the **National Cultural Centre** in February
3. Culminating in the **Children's Costume and Road Parade** through Georgetown

**Categories:** Dramatic Poetry, Calypso, Dance, Masquerade, Hip Hop, Jingle, Costume and Physical Display, plus the Costume/Float Parade.

**Age bands:** 5–7, 8–10, 11–13, and 14–17/18 — so there's a category for a student at any point from nursery through Form 6.

**Scale:** Nearly **1,500 students from all eleven education districts** in the 2026 edition; roughly **2,500 students** in the 2025 parade. Region 2's calypso and dramatic poetry rounds alone drew 14 and 23 schools respectively in one year.

**Run by:** The Ministry of Education's **Unit of Allied Arts**. Organisation has modernised — 2026 introduced a **digital scoring system** for faster, more accurate results.

**Hinterland access is explicitly funded:** The Unit of Allied Arts **provides meals and accommodation** for hinterland students competing in the national finals in Georgetown. Students from Region 7, Region 8, and deep-Rupununi communities like **Karaudarnau** have competed at nationals. This is one of the few national opportunities where the travel barrier is actively removed rather than just acknowledged.

**What winning looks like:** Real named recognition. 2026 winners included **Atiya Mohamed of Zeeburg Secondary (Region 3)** topping 14–18 Calypso with *"Hands Off Guyana"*; **Kareem Jabor of Christ Church Secondary** winning Dramatic Poetry with *"Death of Masquerade"*; **Zelie James of Santa Rosa Primary (Region 1)** first in 8–10 Calypso; **Amia Peters of No. 29 Primary (Region 5)** winning Dramatic Poetry.

**Framing to give students:** Education Minister Sonia Parag has tied this directly to the **"Orange Economy"** — government's push to develop culture as a national product — and told participants plainly: *don't ever be shy about your talent.* The Ministry positions the arts as developing emotional intelligence, critical thinking, and cultural awareness, not as a diversion from academics.

## Institute of Creative Arts schools ✅
Formal training, mostly free/government-run, generally **age 17+**, with **no CSEC requirement for several programmes**:
- **E.R. Burrowes School of Art** — Drawing, Painting, Sculpture, Ceramics, Jewellery, Photography, Textile & Fashion Design, Leather Art
- **National School of Dance**
- **National School of Drama and Theatre Arts**
- **National School of Music** — currently **being rebuilt** as part of government's creative-industry strategy; confirm current intake status

## Guyana Cultural & Creative Industries Registry ✅
A **national directory of Guyanese artistes and cultural workers** maintained by the Ministry of Culture, Youth and Sport (**gncr.mcys.gov.gy**). For a young creative, getting registered is a concrete, actionable step toward being visible to the sector — worth surfacing as a task, not just information.

## Culture & Creative Industries Grant 🟡
The Ministry of Culture, Youth and Sport operates a **Culture & Creative Industries Grant**. Details weren't retrievable in this pass — confirm current eligibility, amounts, and windows directly with the Ministry.

## Guyana Festival, One Guyana Concert ✅
Annual government-organised events explicitly described as creating **opportunities for local artistes to grow**, including a **cookout competition** at Guyana Festival 2026. Lower-stakes, higher-visibility entry points for young creatives than formal competition.

# CATEGORY 4: SPORTS

## National Secondary Schools Track and Field Championships ("Nationals") ✅
**The main national schools sporting event**, and it was significantly restructured recently — details matter here.

**How it works now:**
- Held at the **National Track and Field Centre, Leonora**, over **three days in March** (moved from November)
- **Track and field only** — swimming and cycling were dropped from the revamped format
- Roughly **2,000 student-athletes** compete for their **school districts**
- **Regional qualification rounds happen first** — the national event is the final stage, so regional meets are the actual entry point
- Now organised directly by the **Ministry of Education** (taking over from the Guyana Teachers' Union), with a management committee including the **Athletics Association of Guyana**, teachers, and Fire Service and Police representatives
- Sponsored by the Government of Guyana with **Banks DIH** as main beverage sponsor

**Why the date change matters — tell students this:** The move from November to March was deliberate, to stop athletes **missing qualification windows for the CARIFTA Games** and other international events. Under the old calendar, athletes peaked too late to be channelled into regional competition. So Nationals is now explicitly positioned as a **stepping stone to CARIFTA and international athletics**, not an end point. A student who performs in March has a live pathway onward that same year.

## ExxonMobil Guyana Schools' Kayaking Championship ✅
A newer, less crowded competition — worth flagging precisely *because* fewer students know about it. Multi-leg format (an Inter Schools Championship leg plus a national segment) staged on the **Demerara River**, competing by **district**. **District 4 (East Coast Demerara)**, driven by **President's College**, took a recent national title with 60 points. Organised with Dr. Deon Anderson; ExxonMobil community relations involved.

## Cricket pathway ✅
- **Republic Bank / Cricket West Indies "Five For Fun"** — a grassroots project explicitly aimed at building interest in cricket at an early age, with the hope participants aspire to the highest level
- **Scotiabank Kiddy Cricket** for the youngest tier
- **Guyana Cricket Board / West Indies Grassroots Program** — ages 11–17 across Regions 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 10
- Progression: school/club → county (Berbice, Demerara, Essequibo) → national youth teams → **West Indies Academy** → senior West Indies team
- No CSEC gatekeeping at any stage

## Football pathway ✅
**Guyana Football Federation** academy programmes and private academies (e.g. Vurlon Mills Football Academy, ages 4–16) → **Elite League** (top domestic tier) → national youth teams → senior **Golden Jaguars**. Women's pathway is active — the **Lady Jags** have competed at the **CONCACAF Women's U-20 Championship**.

## Swimming and other individual sports ✅
Guyana competes at the **Central American and Caribbean Games** — **Raekwon Noel** won gold in the Men's 200m Individual Medley at the 2026 edition. Since swimming was dropped from schools' Nationals, the route now runs through **club and association structures** rather than the school system — an important practical distinction for a swimmer, and worth stating clearly so they don't wait for a school competition that no longer exists.

## Sports administration and management ✅
Not just for athletes. The **Ministry of Culture, Youth and Sport** and **National Sports Commission** ran a two-day sports management seminar (July 2026, Arthur Chung Conference Centre) with **Deakin University** (home to a globally #2-ranked Sports Management Master's) and the ExxonMobil Guyana Global Super League — **explicitly open to students** alongside federations and industry professionals, covering governance, workforce development, event leveraging and sports tourism. For a student who loves sport but isn't going pro, this is the adjacent career door.

# CATEGORY 5: LEADERSHIP, SERVICE & CIVIC PARTICIPATION

## President's Youth Award Republic of Guyana (PYARG) ✅
**Guyana's equivalent of the Duke of Edinburgh's Award** — and probably the single most underrated opportunity in this entire document, because it's non-competitive.

**How it works:**
- **Ages 14–25**; running since **1998**
- Deliberately **"balanced, non-competitive"** — you're not beating other students, you're meeting a standard. Enormous advantage for a student who doesn't thrive in competition.
- **Four sections:** Community Service, Physical Recreation, Skills Training, and **Adventurous Journeys**
- **Three levels:** **Bronze (6 months), Silver (12 months), Gold (18 months)**
- Each participant works with an **Award Leader or Assessor**, and is encouraged to examine their own interests, abilities and ambitions and set their own challenges — it's structured self-discovery, which makes it a natural companion to a career-planning site

**The Adventurous Journeys, concretely:** Bronze participants have done a **15-mile hike** (Linden and Essequibo); Silver a **30-mile hike** (Bartica area); Gold a **60-mile hike through the Rupununi savannahs**. One year saw **700+ participants** across the journeys — 630 Bronze, 160 Silver, 35 Gold. The stated purpose goes beyond the award: it gives participants a chance to **see their own country** and build resilience outside their comfort zone.

**Inclusion:** PYARG has extended to young people with disabilities, starting with the **Open Doors Centre** ('C' Field, Sophia) and the **Gifted Hands Learning Centre for Special Needs** (Kingston). Its stated aim is to reach all young people regardless of neighbourhood, religion, or political background.

**Honest note:** Officials have acknowledged **declining numbers at Silver and Gold** levels while working to raise standards, and that not everyone is willing to meet the protocols. Translation for students: **completing Gold is genuinely distinguishing**, precisely because most don't.

## Youth Parliament (Parliament of Guyana) ✅
Ten-plus years of operation, run by the **Parliament of Guyana** (parliament.gov.gy/youthparliament). Alumni are recognised with awards across four categories: **Leadership Excellence, Community Impact, Advocacy Champion, and Innovative Solutions** — indicating what the programme values and what participation can earn you. Direct, structured exposure to the actual legislative process; the obvious starting point for any student interested in law, politics, or public policy.

## CARICOM Youth Ambassador Programme (CYAP) ✅
Launched 1993, formally instituted 1994, now 30+ years old. Youth Ambassadors **advocate for and educate young people** on issues defined through CARICOM, the **Commonwealth Youth Programme**, and the **UN General Assembly** — and have represented the region at genuinely high levels, including a Youth Ambassador delivering an intervention at the **UN ECOSOC Youth Forum**.

**Particularly relevant to Guyana:** the **CARICOM Secretariat is headquartered at Turkeyen, Greater Georgetown** — the regional body is physically in Guyana, which should make access easier than for youth in other member states. A recurring CARICOM concern is that young people don't know enough about the **CSME (CARICOM Single Market and Economy)** — the mechanism that lets a Guyanese graduate work in another member state without unnecessary barriers. That's directly relevant to any student weighing regional career mobility.

## Volunteer Youth Corps (VYC) ✅
A long-established Guyanese NGO (vycguyana.org / vycguyana.net) positioning itself as the premier health, social and economic youth organisation in the country.

**What it runs:**
- **Big Brothers Big Sisters** — hosted since 2003, mentoring and educational support for disadvantaged children, with STEM integration, in communities including Albouystown and Lodge
- **After-school Interactive Math and Science Program** — ExxonMobil-supported since 2012
- **Annual STEM Conference** (at least seven editions)
- **Youth entrepreneurship support** — business start-up training, ICT training, and mentoring. One intervention trained 15 young entrepreneurs and helped 5 start businesses with **loans up to $1.5 million**, via a revolving fund run with the **Commonwealth Youth Programme** and **Citizens Bank Guyana**, with links to GO-Invest.
- Peer education and workshops with partners including the Guyana Table Tennis Association, Malteenoes Sports Club, and Guyana Junior Chamber

**Also:** VYC hosts **Cuso International** volunteers (6–12 month placements) specifically assisting with **Women Empowerment and Career Guidance** — potentially a direct partner for your site.

## National Youth Corps 🟡
A government youth programme with a long history (first launched 1968, relaunched 2019 at the **Kuru Kuru Training Centre**), built around pillars including employment, initially for **ages 16–35**. **Current status under the present administration was not confirmed in this pass** — verify whether it's still operating before listing it.

## "Top National Youth" recognition 🟡
A **"Top National Youth"** award appears to be conferred (a recent recipient: **Randy Mangal of Skeldon Linepath Secondary School**, via Recovery Guyana). Structure, criteria, and nomination process weren't established in this research — worth chasing, since a named national youth award is exactly the kind of concrete goal to put in front of a student.

# CATEGORY 6: ENTREPRENEURSHIP & INNOVATION

## Tertiary Entrepreneurship Award Programme ✅ *(new — July 2026)*
Run by the **Small Business Bureau (SBB)** with the **Ministry of Education**. The inaugural cohort saw **65+ students from five technical and vocational institutions** complete it.

**How it works:** Practical training in **marketing, financial management, record-keeping, business proposal writing and small business management** → participants develop business proposals → **pitch to a panel of judges** → the **15 most viable proposals each qualify for grants of up to $400,000** to actually establish the business. Over **$8 million** invested in the inaugural round; awards ceremony at the Arthur Chung Conference Centre.

**Why this matters for the site:** it's aimed at **TVET students**, not university students — a rare, high-value opportunity for exactly the cohort usually left out of "entrepreneurship" programming. If your gap engine knows a student is at GTI, ETI, or Carnegie, this should surface automatically.

## Small Business Bureau (general) ✅
Beyond the award programme, the SBB is the standing government vehicle for small-business financing and grants — the practical first stop for any young person trying to register and fund a venture.

## Internationally accessible competitions open to Guyanese students ✅
These need no local institutional sponsor, which makes them viable for a motivated student anywhere in the country:
- **Blue Ocean Student Entrepreneur Competition** — fully **virtual**, global, high-school-aged. 23,000+ students in 2026. **Guyana is explicitly listed among participating countries.** Students pitch business concepts to real entrepreneurs, get feedback, and compete for cash prizes. Registration for the next cycle opens after each year's results.
- **NFTE World Series of Innovation** — **free**, online, **ages 5–24**, built around the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Solo or team entry.
- **Global Youth Entrepreneurship Challenge (GYEC)** — an online **12–24 hour** business-idea sprint for **ages 14–19**, teams of up to 8. Deliverables are a 2-page business plan and a 3-minute video with a prototype or illustration. *(2026 registration has closed; next cycle 2027.)*
- **World Bank Group Youth Summit** — annual, **ages 18–35**, with a **Pitch Competition** and **Hackathon**; the 2026 edition ran hybrid on the theme of jobs and the digital age.
- **Global Student Entrepreneur Awards (GSEA)** — for **university/college students who already own and operate a business**; competes for a share of $100,000.

# HOW THE SITE SHOULD USE THIS

## 1. Adopt the Five Priorities Programme as the tracker
Five slots — sport, instrument, TVET subject, volunteerism, foreign language — is a better engagement model than anything invented from scratch, and it carries Ministry authority. Show a student which slots are filled and suggest specific named opportunities from this document for the empty ones.

## 2. Branch by what the student actually has
- **Grades but no confidence** → Caribbean STEM Olympiads, J.O.F. Haynes debate, National STEAM Fair
- **Talent but not grades** → Children's Mashramani, Institute of Creative Arts schools, sports pathways — *none of these gatekeep on CSEC*
- **Neither yet, wants to build something** → **PYARG Bronze**. It's non-competitive, structured, and available from age 14. This should be close to a default recommendation for a Form 1–3 student with no obvious hook.
- **Hinterland student** → National Scratch Competition (entered from 8 regions), Children's Mashramani (**meals and accommodation funded**), STEMGuyana learning pods, PYARG. Say plainly that students from Port Kaituma, Tabatinga, Santa Rosa and Karaudarnau have competed and won nationally.
- **TVET student** → SBB Tertiary Entrepreneurship Award Programme, ETI/GTI competitions. Note that **Essequibo Technical Institute competes in Mashramani calypso** — TVET students aren't excluded from cultural competition.

## 3. Encode the qualification pipelines, not just the finals
Almost every national event here has a **regional or district qualifying layer that is the real entry point**:
- Mashramani: district → regional → national
- STEAM Fair: regional fairs → biennial national
- Track and Field: regional qualification → March Nationals
- Debate: school entry → knockout rounds

A student told "enter the National STEAM Fair" will miss it. A student told "your regional STEAM fair is the entry point, ask your school now" won't.

## 4. Flag the independent-entry routes prominently
Most students believe they can only compete if their school organises it. Not true, and this is high-leverage information:
- **Caribbean STEM Olympiads** accept **independent competitors unaffiliated with any institution**
- **Blue Ocean, NFTE WSI, GYEC, Cosmos Championship** are all direct-registration and virtual
- **QatarDebate ISDC** explicitly accepts registration from communities without a national championship

## 5. Build a seasonal calendar
Rough annual rhythm from this research (**confirm each year — these shift**):
| When | What |
|---|---|
| **January** | Regional/district Children's Mashramani launches |
| **February** | National Children's Mashramani finals + Road Parade (around Republic Day, Feb 23) |
| **March** | National Secondary Schools Track and Field Championships (Leonora) |
| **April–June** | J.O.F. Haynes Debating Competition (launch late April, final ~June) |
| **July–August** | STEMGuyana intensive holiday programmes; Guyana Festival |
| **Varies** | STEAM Fair (regional then biennial national); Scratch Coding Competition; PYARG (rolling, 6/12/18-month cycles); Kayaking Championship |
